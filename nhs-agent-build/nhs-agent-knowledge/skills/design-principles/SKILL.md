# NHS design principles  

## Purpose  
Ensure healthcare services, products, and processes are patient-centered, inclusive, and effective by aligning with the NHS design principles.  

## When this skill applies  
- Designing NHS services, digital tools, or physical infrastructure.  
- Developing policies, workflows, or systems that impact patient care or staff experience.  
- Collaborating with stakeholders to improve healthcare outcomes.  

## When this skill does not apply  
- Non-NHS contexts (e.g., private healthcare, non-clinical services).  
- Situations where legal, regulatory, or safety requirements explicitly override principles.  

## NHS requirements  
- Adherence to all ten NHS design principles (see source).  
- Integration of patient, carer, and staff perspectives into design decisions.  
- Alignment with NHS values (e.g., equity, transparency, sustainability).  

## Mandatory requirements  
1. **Put people at the heart of everything**  
   - Involve patients, carers, and staff in co-design processes.  
   - Address emotional, physical, and technical needs.  

2. **Design for the outcome**  
   - Define measurable health or wellbeing outcomes.  
   - Ensure designs improve lives directly or indirectly.  

3. **Be inclusive**  
   - Ensure accessibility for people with diverse needs (physical, mental health, cultural, etc.).  

4. **Design for context**  
   - Consider the full patient journey and supporting infrastructure.  

5. **Design for trust**  
   - Prioritize reliability, security, and transparency.  

6. **Test assumptions**  
   - Validate designs with real users and experts.  

7. **Make, learn, iterate**  
   - Prototype, test, and refine solutions.  

8. **Simplify complexity**  
   - Avoid overcomplicating patient journeys.  

9. **Make things open**  
   - Share learning, work, and design decisions.  

10. **Protect the environment**  
    - Minimize environmental impact through sustainable practices.  

## Recommended practices  
- Conduct user research and co-design workshops.  
- Use iterative prototyping and testing.  
- Collaborate with subject matter experts.  
- Document and share design decisions.  

## Do  
- **Engage stakeholders** (patients, carers, staff) in design.  
- **Test with real users** before deployment.  
- **Iterate based on feedback**.  
- **Prioritize inclusivity** in all stages.  
- **Align with measurable outcomes**.  

## Don't  
- **Ignore user feedback** or assumptions.  
- **Design in isolation** without stakeholder input.  
- **Overlook accessibility** or inclusivity.  
- **Compromise security or trust** for efficiency.  
- **Ignore environmental impact** in design decisions.  

## Detailed implementation guidance  
### Principle 1: Put people at the heart of everything  
- **When to use**: Co-designing services, tools, or policies.  
- **Implementation**: Conduct empathy interviews, use personas, and involve stakeholders in workshops.  
- **Accessibility**: Ensure interfaces are usable by people with disabilities (e.g., screen readers, high-contrast modes).  

### Principle 2: Design for the outcome  
- **When to use**: Defining service goals or success metrics.  
- **Implementation**: Define KPIs (e.g., reduced waiting times, improved patient satisfaction).  
- **Accessibility**: Ensure outcomes are measurable for all user groups.  

### Principle 3: Be inclusive  
- **When to use**: Designing services for diverse populations.  
- **Implementation**: Use inclusive language, test with diverse users, and follow WCAG standards.  
- **Accessibility**: Ensure physical and digital services are accessible to people with disabilities.  

### Principle 4: Design for context  
- **When to use**: Mapping end-to-end patient journeys.  
- **Implementation**: Audit existing workflows, identify pain points, and integrate with supporting systems.  
- **Accessibility**: Ensure context-aware design (e.g., mobile-first for remote users).  

### Principle 5: Design for trust  
- **When to use**: Handling sensitive data or high-stakes decisions.  
- **Implementation**: Use encryption, anonymize data, and ensure compliance with GDPR.  
- **Accessibility**: Ensure transparency in data usage and security measures.  

### Principle 6: Test assumptions  
- **When to use**: Validating design concepts.  
- **Implementation**: Conduct usability testing, A/B testing, and peer reviews.  
- **Accessibility**: Test with users who have disabilities.  

### Principle 7: Make, learn, iterate  
- **When to use**: Rapid prototyping or agile development.  
- **Implementation**: Use MVPs, gather feedback, and refine iteratively.  
- **Accessibility**: Ensure prototypes are accessible from the start.  

### Principle 8: Simplify complexity  
- **When to use**: Redesigning complex processes.  
- **Implementation**: Use plain language, reduce steps, and eliminate jargon.  
- **Accessibility**: Ensure simplicity for users with cognitive or literacy challenges.  

### Principle 9: Make things open  
- **When to use**: Sharing knowledge or collaborating across teams.  
- **Implementation**: Publish design decisions, share case studies, and use open-source tools.  
- **Accessibility**: Ensure open resources are accessible to all.  

### Principle 10: Protect the environment  
- **When to use**: Procuring materials or designing infrastructure.  
- **Implementation**: Use sustainable materials, reduce waste, and optimize energy use.  
- **Accessibility**: Ensure environmental policies do not exclude vulnerable groups.  

## Decision rules  
- **Prioritize user needs** over technical constraints.  
- **Balance innovation with safety** (e.g., avoid untested AI in critical care).  
- **Align with NHS values** in all decisions.  

## Accessibility  
- Follow WCAG 2.1 AA/AAA standards for digital services.  
- Ensure physical spaces are wheelchair-accessible and sensory-friendly.  
- Provide alternative formats (e.g., Braille, audio) for non-digital content.  

## Security and data considerations  
- Comply with GDPR and NHS data protection policies.  
- Use secure authentication and encryption for sensitive data.  
- Regularly audit systems for vulnerabilities.  

## Testing and quality gates  
- **User testing**: 100% of designs must be tested with real users.  
- **Peer review**: All designs must be reviewed by at least two subject matter experts.  
- **Compliance check**: Ensure alignment with NHS design principles and legal requirements.  

## Common failure modes  
- **Ignoring user feedback** leading to poor adoption.  
- **Overlooking inclusivity** resulting in exclusion of marginalized groups.  
- **Compromising security** to meet deadlines.  
- **Failing to simplify** complex processes, causing user frustration.  

## Exceptions and deviations  
- **Legal overrides**: If a principle conflicts with law (e.g., mandatory data retention).  
- **Emergency contexts**: Rapid deployment in crisis scenarios (e.g., pandemic response).  
- **Resource constraints**: Limited budget or time for full testing.  
- **Approval required**: Deviations must be documented and approved by NHS leadership.  

## Agent completion checklist  
- [ ] Engaged stakeholders in co-design.  
- [ ] Tested designs with real users.  
- [ ] Addressed inclusivity and accessibility.  
- [ ] Aligned with measurable outcomes.  
- [ ] Ensured security and data compliance.  
- [ ] Documented and shared design decisions.
